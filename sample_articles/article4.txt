# Global Stock Markets Rally as Central Banks Signal Economic Recovery

Stock markets around the world surged to record highs on Monday as major central banks signaled an end to the aggressive interest rate hiking cycle that has defined monetary policy for the past two years. The Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 800 points, closing above 42,000 for the first time in history. European markets followed suit, with the FTSE 100 and DAX both posting gains exceeding 3 percent. The rally was triggered by coordinated statements from the Federal Reserve, European Central Bank, and Bank of Japan indicating that inflation has been brought under control and that rate cuts are likely to begin in the coming months. Federal Reserve Chair stated that economic indicators point to a soft landing, with unemployment remaining low and consumer spending showing resilience. Technology stocks led the gains, with major companies seeing valuations increase by 5 to 8 percent. Analysts predict the bull market could continue through the rest of the year as lower interest rates make borrowing cheaper for businesses and consumers. However, some economists warn that geopolitical risks and supply chain disruptions could still derail the recovery. Bond markets also responded positively, with yields on 10-year Treasury notes falling to their lowest level in 18 months. Emerging markets currencies strengthened against the dollar as investors moved capital into higher-yielding assets.
