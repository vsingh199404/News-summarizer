# India's Space Program Achieves Major Milestone with Mars Colony Mission

The Indian Space Research Organisation announced its most ambitious project to date: a manned mission to establish a permanent research station on Mars by 2040. ISRO Chairman Dr. Rajeev Sharma unveiled the detailed mission plan at a press conference in Bengaluru, describing it as India's greatest scientific endeavor. The mission, codenamed Mangalyaan-3, will involve multiple launches over a five-year period to transport habitat modules, scientific equipment, and crew members to the Martian surface. India's Mars program has already achieved significant milestones, including the successful Mars Orbiter Mission in 2014, which made India the first Asian nation to reach Mars orbit. The estimated cost of the colony mission is 50,000 crore rupees, with funding coming from both government allocation and private sector partnerships. Several Indian technology companies have signed agreements to develop components for the mission, including life support systems and radiation shielding. The crew selection process will begin next year, with candidates required to undergo rigorous physical and psychological testing. International collaboration is also planned, with agreements already in place with NASA and the European Space Agency. The announcement has generated significant excitement within India's scientific community and is expected to inspire a new generation of scientists and engineers.
